# AI Can Write the Code. The Question Is Who Decides What to Write.

Every few months, a new AI coding tool drops and the discourse cycles: *"Will AI replace developers?"*

The answer is nuanced, but the direction is clear: **AI will replace developers who can't decide what to build.**

## The Skill That Compounds

Coding speed is a diminishing advantage. AI is already faster than most humans at translating a clear specification into working code. What AI can't do  yet  is:

- Identify which problem is worth solving
- Navigate organizational ambiguity to find the real requirement
- Make tradeoff decisions with incomplete information
- Know when to stop building and start shipping

These are judgment skills. They compound over a career. And they're exactly the skills that most engineering education ignores.

## What This Means for Engineers

The engineers who thrive in the next decade won't be the fastest coders. They'll be the ones who can:

1. **Frame problems clearly**  so that any tool (human or AI) can execute
2. **Evaluate solutions critically**  knowing that "it works" isn't the same as "it's right"
3. **Direct AI effectively**  treating it as a tool, not a replacement for thinking

## My Bet

I'm betting on clarity. The ability to look at a messy situation, identify the core problem, and articulate a path forward  that's the skill that AI amplifies rather than replaces.

The best prompt is a well-understood problem. Everything else is syntax.
